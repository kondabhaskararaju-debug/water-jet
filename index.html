2. Water Jet Cutting – job-specific fundamentals

What is water jet cutting?

A manufacturing process that uses a high-velocity, high-pressure stream of water to cut material. Abrasive particles may be added when cutting harder materials.

Pure water jet

Uses water without abrasive. Commonly suited to softer materials such as rubber, foam, textiles and some plastics.

Abrasive water jet

Mixes abrasive particles, commonly garnet, into the water stream. It can cut many metals, stone, glass and composites, depending on machine capability and setup.

Main machine components

  • High-pressure pump
  • Water filtration system
  • High-pressure tubing and fittings
  • Cutting head / nozzle
  • Abrasive hopper and feed (if fitted)
  • CNC controller and work table
  • Catch tank

Basic operating sequence

  1. Review drawing, material and job instructions.
  2. Check machine condition and safety devices.
  3. Secure the workpiece correctly.
  4. Load the approved cutting program and verify settings.
  5. Run the job as trained and monitor from a safe position.
  6. Stop safely, inspect the cut and record results.

Factors affecting cut quality

  • Material type and thickness
  • Water pressure and flow
  • Cutting speed
  • Nozzle condition and stand-off distance
  • Abrasive type and feed rate
  • Correct program and workpiece support
High-pressure hazard: A water jet can cause severe injection injuries. Never touch or approach a pressurized jet, search for leaks with your hand, loosen fittings under pressure, or defeat guards/interlocks. Follow site isolation and lockout/tagout procedures.

3. Mechanical Engineering Fundamentals

Engineering mechanics

Force

A push or pull that can change an object's motion or shape. SI unit: newton (N).

Engineering mechanics

Stress

Internal resisting force per unit area. Formula: stress = force ÷ area. SI unit: pascal (Pa).

Engineering mechanics

Strain

Change in length divided by original length. It is dimensionless.

Engineering mechanics

Torque

The turning effect of a force about an axis. For a perpendicular force: torque = force × distance. Unit: N·m.

Materials

Hardness

Resistance of a material to indentation, scratching or wear. Examples of tests include Brinell, Rockwell and Vickers.

Materials

Strength and toughness

Strength is the ability to withstand applied stress. Toughness is the ability to absorb energy before fracture.

Materials

Ductility and brittleness

Ductility is the ability to deform plastically before fracture. Brittle materials fracture with little plastic deformation.

Materials

Common engineering materials

Carbon steel, stainless steel, aluminium alloys, copper alloys and titanium alloys. Selection depends on strength, corrosion resistance, weight, temperature and cost.

Fluid mechanics

Pressure

Force acting per unit area. Formula: pressure = force ÷ area. SI unit: pascal (Pa).

Fluid mechanics

Flow rate

Volume of fluid passing a point per unit time. Common units include L/min and m³/s.

Thermal basics

Heat and temperature

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. Temperature indicates how hot or cold a body is.

Machine elements

Bearing

A machine element that supports a rotating or moving part and reduces friction while carrying load.

Machine elements

Gear

A toothed machine element used to transmit motion and torque between shafts.

Machine elements

Coupling

Connects two shafts to transmit torque. Some couplings accommodate small misalignment.

Drawings

Engineering drawing

A technical representation of a component showing shape, dimensions, tolerances, material and other manufacturing information.

Quality

Tolerance

The permitted variation in a dimension. It defines the acceptable upper and lower limits.

4. Manufacturing processes

Turning

A lathe rotates the workpiece while a cutting tool removes material, commonly to make cylindrical shapes.

Milling

A rotating multi-point cutter removes material from a workpiece to create flat, stepped or contoured surfaces.

Drilling

Produces a round hole using a rotating drill bit.

Grinding

Uses an abrasive wheel to remove small amounts of material and improve surface finish or dimensional accuracy.

Laser cutting

Uses a focused laser beam to cut material. Heat is concentrated in the cutting zone.

Plasma cutting

Uses a high-temperature plasma arc to cut electrically conductive materials.

Water jet vs thermal cutting

Water jet cutting is a cold-cutting process in the sense that it does not use a heat-based cutting source, so it avoids a heat-affected zone typical of thermal processes. Other process effects and edge quality still need inspection.

Surface finish

Describes the texture and roughness of a manufactured surface. It may be specified using parameters such as Ra.

6. Workshop and machine safety

PPE

Use the site-required safety shoes, eye/face protection, hearing protection, gloves where appropriate, and other specified protective equipment. Do not wear loose clothing near moving machinery.

Before starting

Check guards, emergency stop, hoses, fittings, workholding, water supply, abrasive supply (if used), and the work area according to the checklist and SOP.

Emergency stop

Know the location and approved use of the emergency stop. Use it when an immediate hazard occurs, then inform the responsible supervisor.

Housekeeping

Keep floors and walkways clear, manage wet/slippery areas, and dispose of abrasive and waste material as instructed.

7. Interview Questions and Sample Answers

Use these as learning examples. Adapt them honestly to your own experience; do not claim machine experience you do not have.

1. Tell me about yourself.

“I have a background in mechanical engineering and I am interested in manufacturing and machine operations. I understand the importance of following drawings, standard operating procedures, quality checks and workplace safety. I am willing to learn the specific water jet machine and work responsibly as part of the production team.”

2. What is water jet cutting?

“Water jet cutting is a process that uses a high-pressure, high-velocity water stream to cut material. For harder materials, abrasive particles can be mixed with the water to improve cutting ability.”

3. What is the difference between pure and abrasive water jet?

“Pure water jet uses only water and is suitable for many softer materials. Abrasive water jet adds abrasive particles, such as garnet, so it can cut a wider range of harder materials, including metals.”

4. What checks would you do before operating a machine?

“I would follow the machine checklist and SOP, inspect guards and safety devices, check the workpiece and fixtures, verify the approved program and job details, and report any leakage, damage or abnormal condition before starting.”

5. What would you do if you noticed a high-pressure water leak?

“I would keep away from the leak and never use my hand to locate it. I would stop the machine using the approved safe procedure, isolate it as trained, alert the supervisor and ensure it is not restarted until authorized personnel make it safe.”

6. What is tolerance?

“Tolerance is the permitted variation in a dimension. It gives the acceptable upper and lower size limits for a component.”

7. Which instruments do you know?

“I understand the purpose of a vernier caliper, micrometer, steel rule, height gauge and dial indicator. I would select the instrument based on the drawing requirement and its measurement range and accuracy.”

8. Why is safety important in water jet operation?

“The system works at very high pressure. A jet or leak can cause serious injury. Operators must follow SOPs, use required PPE, keep guards and interlocks active, and never handle pressurized components.”

9. What would you do if a cut component is out of tolerance?

“I would identify and segregate the part according to the quality procedure, inform the supervisor or quality team, record the issue and wait for an approved disposition. I would not make unauthorized machine adjustments.”

10. Why should we hire you?

“I am safety-conscious, disciplined and ready to learn. I can follow instructions, work carefully with measurements and drawings, and focus on quality and productivity. I will ask when I am unsure rather than take an unsafe shortcut.”

11. What is preventive maintenance?

“Preventive maintenance is planned inspection and servicing carried out at defined intervals to reduce breakdowns and keep equipment operating reliably.”

12. What would you do if the machine makes an unusual sound?

“I would follow the approved stop procedure, keep clear of moving or pressurized parts, inform the supervisor and not restart until the issue has been checked and permission is given.”
