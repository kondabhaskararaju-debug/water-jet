4. Water jet cutting – the target job

What is it?

A cutting process that directs a very high-pressure, high-speed stream of water through a small orifice onto a material.

Pure water jet

Uses water alone. Often used for softer materials such as foam, rubber, textiles and some plastics.

Abrasive water jet

Mixes abrasive particles, commonly garnet, into the water stream. This enables cutting of many metals, stone, glass and composites, depending on the machine and setup.

Simplified educational sketch. Exact cutting-head design varies by manufacturer. Never dismantle or approach pressurized components.

Main components – understand the function

Cut quality depends on: material and thickness, cutting speed, pressure/flow settings, nozzle condition, abrasive type and feed, stand-off distance, program accuracy and secure workholding. The correct values come from the machine procedure and approved job setup – never guess them.

5. Mechanical fundamentals – learn in simple language

Mechanics

Force

A push or pull that can change motion or shape. Unit: newton (N). Example: pushing a trolley.

Mechanics

Mass and weight

Mass is the amount of matter (kg). Weight is gravitational force: W = m × g, measured in newtons.

Mechanics

Torque

Turning effect of a force. For a perpendicular force: Torque = Force × perpendicular distance. Unit: N·m. Example: opening a tight nut with a spanner.

Mechanics

Stress

Internal force per unit area: Stress = Force / Area. Unit: pascal (Pa). A smaller area under the same force gives higher stress.

Mechanics

Strain

How much a material deforms compared with its original size: Strain = change in length / original length. No unit.

Mechanics

Pressure

Force acting per unit area: Pressure = Force / Area. Unit: pascal. Water jet machines use very high pressure, so safety is critical.

Materials

Hardness

Resistance to indentation, scratching or wear. Common tests: Brinell, Rockwell and Vickers.

Materials

Ductility

Ability to stretch or deform plastically before breaking. Many metals show ductility.

Materials

Brittleness

Tendency to fracture with little plastic deformation. Some cast irons and ceramics are relatively brittle.

Materials

Toughness

Ability to absorb energy before fracture. It combines strength and ductility in the material's response.

Fluids

Flow rate

Volume of fluid passing a point per unit time. Units include L/min or m³/s.

Machine elements

Bearing

Supports a rotating or moving part and reduces friction while carrying load.

Machine elements

Gear

Toothed wheel used to transmit motion and torque between shafts.

Machine elements

Coupling

Connects two shafts to transmit torque. Some types can accommodate small misalignment.

Design

Factor of safety

A design margin between a material's failure capacity and the working requirement. The appropriate value depends on design rules and loading conditions.

Design

Engineering drawing

A technical communication document showing views, dimensions, tolerances, material and manufacturing requirements.

6. Manufacturing processes

Turning

Workpiece rotates while a cutting tool removes material. Used for cylindrical parts.

Milling

A rotating multi-edge cutter removes material to create flat, slots or contoured surfaces.

Drilling

A rotating drill creates a round hole in a workpiece.

Grinding

Abrasive wheel removes a small amount of material to improve size accuracy and surface finish.

Welding

Joins materials, usually metals, using heat, pressure or both. Resume lists welding as part of Go-Kart fabrication.

Sheet metal forming

Changes the shape of sheet material by bending, punching, drawing or other processes.

Laser / plasma cutting

Thermal cutting methods that use concentrated heat. They can create a heat-affected zone.

Water jet cutting

Uses a high-speed water stream and does not rely on a heat-based cutting source; it avoids the typical thermal heat-affected zone.

8. Water jet and workshop safety

High-pressure danger

A water jet or pinhole leak can cause severe injection injury. Never touch a suspected leak or search for it with a hand.

Before operation

Follow the checklist: inspect guards, emergency stop, hoses, fittings, workholding, water quality and abrasive supply if fitted.

Isolation

Never loosen a fitting or service a pressure line while pressurized. Follow the site's isolation and lockout/tagout procedure and only work when authorized.

PPE and housekeeping

Use required safety footwear, eye/face and hearing protection and other site-specified PPE. Keep the floor clear and manage wet/slippery areas.

Interview-ready response to a leak: “I will stay clear, never touch or inspect the leak by hand, stop and isolate the machine using the approved procedure if safe and trained to do so, alert the supervisor, and wait for authorized maintenance personnel.”

9. Interview questions with easy answers

1. Tell me about yourself.

Use the introduction above. Keep it to about 45–60 seconds and speak naturally.

2. What is water jet cutting?

It is a cutting process using a high-pressure, high-velocity water stream. Abrasive particles can be added to cut harder materials.

3. Difference between pure and abrasive water jet?

Pure water uses only water and is suitable for many soft materials. Abrasive water jet adds particles such as garnet to cut many harder materials.

4. Why is water jet called a cold cutting process?

Because the cutting action is not produced by a thermal heat source like laser or plasma. It avoids the typical heat-affected zone of thermal cutting.

5. What is pressure?

Pressure is force per unit area. Its SI unit is pascal (Pa).

6. What is tolerance?

Tolerance is the permitted variation in a component's dimension, defined by upper and lower limits.

7. What is GD&T?

It is a system on engineering drawings that specifies allowable geometric variation such as position, flatness, perpendicularity and runout.

8. What is the function of a bearing?

It supports a moving or rotating part, carries load and reduces friction.

9. What is the difference between welding and machining?

Welding joins parts. Machining removes material to obtain the required shape or dimension.

10. How is your Go-Kart project relevant to this role?

“It gave me exposure to mechanical design, fabrication, assembly, testing and troubleshooting. I understand the importance of following a design, checking fit and function, and working safely as a team. I want to apply that foundation while learning water jet operation.”

11. Do you have water jet operating experience?

“I have not listed professional water jet operating experience. My background is in mechanical design and Go-Kart fabrication. I understand the basic water jet principle and safety requirements, and I am ready to learn the machine-specific operating procedure under proper training.”

12. What if a finished component is out of tolerance?

Segregate or identify the part according to the quality procedure, inform the supervisor or quality team, record the nonconformity and wait for an approved decision. Do not make unauthorized machine changes.

13. What if the machine makes an unusual sound?

Follow the approved stop procedure, keep clear of hazardous parts, inform the supervisor and do not restart until it has been checked and permission is given.

14. Why should we select you?

“I have a mechanical engineering foundation, CAD knowledge and practical project exposure in fabrication and testing. I am disciplined, safety-conscious and willing to learn. I will follow instructions and focus on quality and teamwork.”
